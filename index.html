THE FIELD GUIDE

Know the name.
Check the evidence.

Fat loss, muscle gain, and recovery are very different outcomes. A peptide's popularity, a hormone change, and a proven clinical benefit are three different things.

Categories reflect areas of interest, not proven benefits. U.S. context · Checked September 22, 2026.

01 / Fat loss & metabolic health

Semaglutide and tirzepatide are ingredients in approved prescription medicines for specific uses. Retatrutide is investigational. Tesamorelin has a specific HIV-associated lipodystrophy indication; its label says it is not indicated for weight-loss management.

These distinctions matter more than a storefront category. This demo does not offer branded medicines or approve generic research vials. FDA: GLP-1 products ↗ · Tesamorelin label ↗

02 / Muscle gain & growth-hormone signaling

CJC-1295 and ipamorelin are often discussed in this category. Human CJC-1295 studies measured growth hormone and IGF-1. Those results do not demonstrate safe muscle growth or improved athletic performance.

FDA has identified safety concerns and evidence gaps for these compounds. We do not make muscle-gain claims or provide dosing, injection, or stacking instructions. CJC-1295 study ↗ · FDA safety information ↗

03 / Recovery & experimental compounds

BPC-157, TB-500-related fragments, AOD-9604, and MOTS-c attract interest for recovery or metabolism. Available evidence does not establish the advertised human benefits or a safe self-injection regimen.

FDA identifies compound-specific concerns including immune reactions, impurities, and missing safety data. A fragment, a full-length peptide, and a similarly named analog are not interchangeable. Review individual compounds ↗

04 / What “approved” actually means

FDA approval applies to a particular finished medicine and its labeled uses. It does not transfer to every product containing a similarly named ingredient. Compounding policy is also separate from drug approval.

“Research use only” labeling does not override evidence that a product is being sold for human use. FDA warning-letter example ↗

05 / About this storefront concept

All listings, prices, and packaging are illustrative. There is no inventory, supplier, pharmacy, prescription service, purity certificate, or payment processing behind FORM. The bag is a visual demo only.

A real launch would need a defined jurisdiction, lawful product and prescribing model, qualified suppliers, appropriate manufacturing and dispensing controls, and substantiated claims. The catalog is a design starting point, not a list of products cleared for general sale.
